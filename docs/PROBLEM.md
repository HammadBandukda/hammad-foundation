## 2 · The problems

### P1 · The number problem (primary)
The series makes the emblem the round part of the number. **11 has no round part.** Placing the emblem beside "11" reads as "110", which is the mistake on their own competition poster.

The precedent shows why it matters: Vignelli's Abdi İbrahim 100th mark and Pentagram's MTA 40th both work because the anniversary number grows out of the existing mark's geometry rather than being placed next to it (`docs/RESEARCH.md` §2).

### P2 · The system problem
Every year, sign and campaign looks different: new wordmarks, new colours, new layouts, and no lockup rules. Each anniversary mark has been a one-off, used for a year and then dropped. Nothing carries over.

### P3 · The production problem
The 10 Years mark relies on 3D gold, gradients and a thin script. It breaks:
- on **hi-vis vests and T-shirts** (screen print, often one colour)
- in **embroidery** (no gradients, no hairlines)
- on **stickers, badges and favicons** (tiny sizes)
- on **deep-green signboards** (the green disappears)

These are exactly where a volunteer organisation appears most: clean-ups with 700–4,000 volunteers, Roti Bank, Ramadan camps.

### P4 · The brand-architecture problem
They run 12+ programmes under four pillars, and each programme sign is designed from scratch (a blue Medical Complex, a green Skill Valley, a white water plaque). There is no shared template that links a programme back to the parent.

### P5 · The language gap (required: an Urdu version is a deliverable)
The audience is largely Urdu-speaking, yet every mark is English-only. The site's warmest moments (جاری سفر) are in Urdu. Urdu ۱۱ is two vertical strokes, so one mark can read in both scripts.

## 3 · Problem statement

> **Continue Hammad Foundation's anniversary series at a number with no circle in it, as a simple, flat mark that works the same on a favicon, a volunteer's vest and a billboard, and that shows how one emblem can hold together everything the foundation does.**

## 5 · The "system thinking" page (option 1, agreed 5 Nov)

The 11 Years mark stays the focus. One PDF page shows the wider idea:

- **Parent:** the full emblem, unchanged.
- **Pillar icons:** each quadrant lifted out as its own mark (Food, Skills, Water, Health), in the same line style and the same greens.
- **Programme lockup:** parent plus pillar icon plus programme name, e.g. *Hammad Foundation · Roti Bank*. One signboard template where only the icon and name change.
- **On the page:** the 11 Years mark, the four pillar icons, and one programme signboard before and after.

**Precedents:**
- **UN Sustainable Development Goals:** one circular colour wheel made of segments. Each of the 17 goals has its own icon and colour and is used on its own, while the wheel stays the parent ([UN SDG guidelines, Sep 2023](https://www.un.org/sustainabledevelopment/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/E_SDG_Guidelines_Sep20238.pdf), [communications materials](https://www.un.org/sustainabledevelopment/news/communications-material/)). This is the closest match to a quartered emblem.
- **Rotary, Areas of Focus:** one master logo plus a set of matching cause icons. Each has its own colour, can be grouped in a row or a circle, has black and white versions, and is always shown alongside the club logo ([Rotary Brand Center](https://brandcenter.rotary.org/en-us/our-brand/brand-elements/logos-and-graphics/areas-of-focus-icons), [programme logos](https://brandcenter.rotary.org/en-us/our-brand/brand-elements/logos-and-graphics/logos-for-rotary-programs-and-activities)).
- **Akhuwat (Pakistan):** an endorsed model, where every programme carries the parent name.

**Out of scope for the competition:** full sub-brand guidelines and a redesign of the master logo. Keep both for the portfolio case study or a follow-up pitch.
