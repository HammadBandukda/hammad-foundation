Four ways to say 11 Years

First sketches for the anniversary mark that sits beside the existing logo. Each direction keeps the emblem, the gold numerals and the “Years” ribbon so it reads as the sequel to the 10 Years mark.

  • Emblem unchanged
  • Gold and green palette
  • “Years” ribbon
  • Legible at 70 px

The problem. In the 10 Years mark the round emblem stands in for the 0. “11” has no zero, and putting the emblem after “11” reads as “110”. Every direction below gives the emblem a new job.

Direction A

Two Pillars

Two gold 1s stand on either side of the emblem like a gateway, or two hands holding it up.

Story
Hath mai hath: the foundation held between two hands.
How it reads
Symmetric and calm, with the strongest sense of a crest.
Watch out
With the emblem in the middle it can read as “101”. The 1s need to touch the emblem, not sit apart like digits.

concepts/svg/A-two-pillars.svg

Direction B

Rising Skyline

The emblem sits in front and the two 1s rise behind it, side by side, like towers or a growth chart.

Story
Upward growth toward “Transforming Pakistan by 2050”.
How it reads
The 1s stay next to each other, so “11” reads first and the emblem never sits in a digit slot.
Watch out
The emblem covers the lower half of the numerals, so the 1s must be tall enough to show their flags clearly at small sizes.

concepts/svg/B-rising-skyline.svg

Direction C

Seal on the Step

The second 1 is a step taller than the first, and the emblem sits on top of it as a seal.

Story
One more step up from the 10 Years mark.
How it reads
A clear “11” with a rising rhythm; the emblem works like the dot on an i and becomes the visual crown.
Watch out
The smallest emblem of the four, so its icons blur first at favicon size.

concepts/svg/C-seal-on-the-step.svg

Direction D

Ribbon Wrap

The emblem keeps its 10 Years position and size. A gold ring frames it, and the ribbon carries “11 Years”.

Story
The closest sibling to the 10 Years mark, so the two read as a series.
How it reads
The emblem is the hero again; the number lives on the ribbon, so it can never read as “110”.
Watch out
The “11” is smaller than in A–C, so the number is less dominant from a distance.
